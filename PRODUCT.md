# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain HTML/CSS — single static page, no build step, opens in any browser and hosts free on GitHub Pages or Netlify. Chosen by the user for simplicity.

## Users

Primary: senior high school students (and teachers) at school in the Philippines, in the middle of a hot school day. Their job: find something cold, sweet, and affordable to beat the heat — and decide in seconds whether to buy.

Secondary: teachers and visitors viewing the page as a business pitch — judges or evaluators assessing the product idea, its layers, and its pricing.

## Product Purpose

A chilled layered dessert sold at school: mango float layered on top with leche flan, with two optional drizzles (chocolate and melted sugar). The heat index in the Philippines makes cold desserts especially in demand; this product offers a refreshing, cooling sensation for students and teachers alike. The distinct layers are the product's signature — visibly different from single-texture desserts.

Success means: buyers understand the product and its layers at a glance, know the price immediately, and can order through the real channels (messaging or walk-up at school). The page doubles as a business pitch that presents the concept well to teachers/judges.

## Positioning

A layered chilled dessert built for the Philippine heat: mango float's creamy-graham layers finished with leche flan on top — two desserts in one cup. A neighboring product (plain mango float, plain leche flan, halo-halo cups, ice candy) cannot truthfully claim this specific combination, and the visible layers make the difference provable at a glance.

## Operating Context

- Sold at a school in the Philippines; buyers are students and teachers on campus during the school day.
- Ordering happens through messaging (e.g., chat) and walk-up purchase at school. The page's checkout section is a mock — for show only — and must not present itself as a working payment system.
- Real prices: ₱30 without drizzle; ₱35 with both melted sugar and chocolate drizzle.
- Hot-weather demand (heat index) is the core sales argument.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- One product line with two price points: no drizzle ₱30 / with both drizzles ₱35.
- Drizzles: chocolate and melted sugar, offered together as the upgrade option.
- Product photography does not exist yet; the user will add real photos after the site is built. The build must make swapping in photos trivial (clearly-marked image slots, simple file names, instructions in code comments).
- A mock checkout/order section is required, explicitly for show — it must be visually labeled as a demo and must not submit real payments or fake a live ordering system. Real ordering routes to messaging and walk-up.
- The user may want to fill in details later (e.g., chat handle for messaging orders) — keep those as clearly-marked editable spots too.

## Brand Commitments

- Product/business name: **Mango Leche Flan** (as given by the user).
- Voice: friendly, school-appropriate, appetizing — it should make a thirsty student stop scrolling.

## Evidence on Hand

- No product photos yet (user plans to add them soon — build must accommodate).
- No logo, no testimonials, no press. Do not fabricate any of these.
- Confirmed pricing and product composition (above) are real facts from the seller.

## Product Principles

1. **Appetite first.** Every design decision should make the dessert look colder, sweeter, and more layered — the page must trigger craving, especially for someone in the heat.
2. **Price in plain sight.** ₱30/₱35 are a core selling point (affordable for students); never bury them.
3. **Show the layers.** The visible layering is the differentiation; the design should make the layers legible, not hide them in generic food photography tropes.
4. **Honest by design.** Mock checkout is labeled as demo; real orders route to messaging/walk-up; no invented testimonials or claims.
5. **Easy to grow.** Photo slots, prices, and contact details are simple for a non-developer student to edit later.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Audience includes students browsing on phones (likely primary device) — mobile-first layout.
- Text contrast and tap targets sized for real use; page readable outdoors/bright light.
